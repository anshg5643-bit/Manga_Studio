How it works, and what it can't do.

The pipeline

1. OCR. RapidOCR (PaddleOCR PP-OCR weights in ONNX) returns text lines and boxes on the CPU.

2. Balloons. Line boxes are painted white on a thresholded copy of the page. Connected white regions are balloons. Lines in one region are joined. Lines on the art stay as free blocks.

3. Keep filter. Twenty geometric and OCR features (balloon shape, position, text size, confidence, local whiteness) feed a gradient-boosted classifier. It decides story text versus sound effects, page numbers and signs. Until you train it on labels, a hand-written rule is used instead.

4. Cleanup. Merged words are split, digit/letter confusions fixed, ".." becomes "...".

5. Reading order. An XY-cut on balloon boxes forms tiers (top to bottom) and columns (right to left). When nothing can be cut, a weighted top-right-first score is used.

6. Speakers. Baselines only: alternating characters, or clustering of a small grey-level descriptor of the art around each balloon. Rectangular tail-less boxes become NARRATION. Fix mistakes on the Speakers page.

Output format

One JSON object per sequence: {"sequence_id": "...", "pages": [[{"speaker":"char1","text":"..."}], [...], [...]]}. Three page lists, lines in reading order, NARRATION only for narrator text.

Known limits

Punctuation-only balloons such as ... or ?! are often missed by OCR. Speaker labelling is a baseline and will mislabel when one character speaks twice in a row. Narration detection is a shape rule. Always review results in the Analyzer.

Terms and privacy

This is a local prototype. Accounts and analyses are stored in a SQLite file on the machine running the server. Passwords are hashed. Pages you upload are processed locally and never sent to a hosted AI service.
